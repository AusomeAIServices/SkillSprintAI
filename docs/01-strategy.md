# Product strategy

## Vision and positioning

Make practical AI capability attainable in a working day without requiring people to become technologists. SkillSprint AI is a practice and evidence platform: a learner finishes with something useful and can explain why it is trustworthy.

The promise is **“Build a useful AI habit in 15 minutes a day.”** Avoid promising expertise, job readiness, guaranteed productivity gains, or mastery in one day. “ChatGPT in a Day” is an introductory bootcamp comprising eight 15-minute lessons, not a mastery credential.

The founder hypothesis is that people struggle less with access to AI explanations than with selecting a relevant task, practising safely, judging an answer, and repeating the practice. Validate this through interviews and observed tasks before making market claims.

## Audiences and jobs to be done

| Audience | Starting problem | Desired outcome | Example evidence | Route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individuals | AI feels unfamiliar or unreliable | Ask clearer daily questions and check answers | A checked travel-planning checklist | Everyday AI |
| Information workers | Repetitive writing and information overload | Produce reusable work drafts and summaries | Meeting action table with source references | AI at Work |
| Nontechnical professionals | General demos do not fit their field | Apply AI within professional judgment and confidentiality boundaries | Client brief with facts, assumptions, and review notes | Professional Practice |
| Executives | Hype obscures investment decisions | Select use cases, measure value, assign accountability | Pilot charter and risk/value scorecard | Lead with AI |
| IT professionals | Need maintainable, controlled adoption | Use Codex to inspect, change, test, and review code | Tested change with a reviewable diff | Build with Codex |
| AI practitioners | Demos fail under real workloads | Build evaluated AI apps and bounded agent workflows | App with evaluation report and run trace | Build AI Systems |

Roles shape examples and prerequisites; they are not fixed ability labels. A finance professional can enter the technical route after demonstrating the prerequisites. Learners may change roles without losing their records.

## Launch wedge and sequencing

Start with information workers in small service businesses and everyday users entering through ChatGPT in a Day. This gives the same lesson engine a personal and workplace test while keeping content production manageable. Recruit 20–30 adult pilot learners, including beginners and users with accessibility needs; this is discovery, not a statistically representative market sample.

Offer all six pathways in the product vision. Only the first two are complete launch courses. Mark future pathways “planned” with a syllabus preview; never place a purchase or completion promise against unavailable content.

Differentiation hypotheses to validate against free tutorials, general course libraries, and internal training:

1. Role-relevant practice makes the first result useful quickly.
2. A private portfolio makes progress visible beyond a quiz score.
3. Retrieval practice and later transfer checks distinguish recall from capability.
4. Source dates and change tracking reduce stale product instructions.
5. Transparent AI feedback teaches judgment without presenting a model as an examiner of record.

This is not a researched competitor comparison. Conduct named competitor and pricing research before launch positioning is finalized.

## Offering and monetization experiments

All figures below are proposed USD prices for testing, not current competitor prices or OpenAI fees. External ChatGPT/Codex access is separate; do not imply a learning subscription includes those products.

| Offer | Proposed experiment | Included value |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | Free | Eight foundation lessons, basic portfolio, static hints |
| Individual | Test $12 vs $19/month | Workplace path, review scheduling, expanded practice, bounded coaching |
| Practitioner, later | Test $29/month | Technical learning paths; lab compute has a separate allowance |
| Teams, later | Test $18–25/seat/month with a minimum | Cohorts, assignments, aggregate outcomes, administration |

MVP pilot uses manual entitlements and no checkout. Add subscriptions only after activation, retention, and perceived value are demonstrated. Start with a hosted payment flow when implemented; never store card details.

Illustrative unit economics at a $15 blended monthly revenue per paying learner: assume $1.50 AI, $0.75 hosting, $0.75 payments, and $1.50 variable support costs. Contribution is $10.50 or 70%, before salaries, content development, acquisition, tax, and fixed overhead. At $4 AI cost it falls to $8 or 53%. These assumptions must be replaced with observed costs; payment fees are placeholders.

Use: contribution = net revenue − AI − compute/storage − payment fees − variable support. CAC payback months = acquisition cost / monthly contribution; at an assumed $30 CAC and $10.50 contribution this is about 2.9 months, excluding churn effects. Do not present this as a forecast.

## Acquisition and retention

Use the free one-day course as the entry offer. Recruit pilot cohorts through founder-led workshops and willing business partners, with explicit permission for communications. Publish before/after task examples using synthetic information. Offer a voluntary skill evidence share card that reveals no private portfolio content.

Retention comes from an achievable weekly goal, the next useful task, and reviewing prior skills. Use opt-in reminders with quiet hours and a timezone setting. Missing a day never deletes achievements or incurs a punitive streak reset. Team managers see assigned completion and aggregate competency results; private prompts and artifacts remain private unless deliberately shared.

## Ninety-day validation plan

| Period | Experiment | Decision signal (proposed gate) |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–14 | 12 learner interviews and 5 team-buyer interviews; prototype task observations | At least 8 learners identify a repeated task and demonstrate the current difficulty |
| Days 15–35 | Concierge foundation course, 20–30 learners | At least 60% complete first practice; median first result within 20 minutes including onboarding |
| Days 36–60 | MVP cohort; compare baseline and unseen task after 7 days | At least 60% of assessed learners pass transfer; report attrition and sample size |
| Days 61–90 | Workplace course and willingness-to-pay test | At least 35% week-four retained practice; at least 10 explicit purchase commitments before adding billing |

Report raw counts and cohort definitions alongside percentages. If learners finish quizzes but fail transfer, revise instruction before adding gamification. If retention is weak, interview dropouts before buying acquisition. If coaching exceeds the proposed $1.50 monthly allowance, reduce unnecessary calls and test value; do not silently degrade assessment.

## Business risks and owners

| Risk | Response | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Product instructions become stale | Monthly source review and immediate review on a known feature change | Curriculum lead |
| Generic content has low value | Observe target-role work, author authentic tasks, measure transfer | Founder + learning designer |
| AI cost exceeds revenue | Per-user budgets, cheaper evaluated configuration, static fallback | CTO |
| Enterprise needs consume the MVP | Defer SSO, LMS and integrations until a validated buying requirement | Founder |
| Credential overclaims | Call awards completion badges; retain evidence and rubric version | Learning lead |
| Vendor dependency | Own curriculum, skill graph and evaluation fixtures; use explicit adapters later | Architect |

Initial planning assumes a founder/product lead, one full-stack engineer, a part-time learning designer, and shared design/QA support, assisted by Codex. Delivery timing is a planning range, not a commitment; agent count is not a substitute for human review or subject expertise.
